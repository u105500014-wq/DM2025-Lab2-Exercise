So, what exactly is Machine Learning? At its core, Machine Learning is a subfield of artificial intelligence that focuses on enabling systems to learn from data without being explicitly programmed. Instead of writing specific instructions for every possible scenario, we provide algorithms with data, and they learn patterns and make predictions or decisions.

[TRANSCRIPT - 0:45]
Why is this important? Machine Learning is revolutionizing various industries. Think about personalized recommendations on streaming services, spam filters in your email, fraud detection in banking, and even self-driving cars. These are all powered by Machine Learning algorithms.

[SLIDE DESCRIPTION]
Slide 4: Types of Machine Learning - Supervised, Unsupervised, Reinforcement.

[TRANSCRIPT - 1:05]
There are broadly three main types of Machine Learning: Supervised Learning, Unsupervised Learning, and Reinforcement Learning. We'll delve into each of these in more detail in subsequent lectures, but for now, let's briefly touch upon them.

[TRANSCRIPT - 1:15]
Supervised learning involves training a model on a labeled dataset, meaning the input data is paired with the correct output. Unsupervised learning, on the other hand, deals with unlabeled data, where the algorithm must find patterns on its own. Reinforcement learning is about training an agent to make a sequence of decisions by rewarding desired behaviors and penalizing undesired ones.
